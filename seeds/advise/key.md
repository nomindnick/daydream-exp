These notes are for the judge only. They record what an experienced California public-agency construction lawyer knows about this file on Monday morning, before Corvane's response is in.

**Ground 1, licensing.** Two halves, and they come out differently.
- HVAC: the protest is wrong. A general building contractor may do or superintend any part of a multi-trade prime contract itself (B&P § 7057(a), (b)). The code names no HVAC exception. A Class B may self-perform the rooftop units.
- Fire sprinklers: the protest has a point. A Class B may not contract for a project that includes a fire protection system unless it holds the classification (C-16) or subcontracts the work to a contractor that does (§ 7057(c), § 7026.12). Corvane's bid, on its face, does neither.
- The label matters. The ITB (§ 7.1) required a Class B and nothing else, and Corvane holds it. Section 7028.15(e) requires the agency to verify before award that the contractor was "properly licensed" when it bid; no case settles whether a missing specialty classification makes a bid nonresponsive on its face or raises a responsibility question. Whether to reject turns on facts outside the bid: what Corvane's qualifying individual holds personally, what is pending at the licensing board, what the firm would do about the sprinkler work. A rejection built on facts outside the bid is a responsibility finding in everything but name, and a responsibility finding requires notice, the evidence, and a chance to respond (D.H. Williams; Great West; Boydston). Rejecting Corvane as "nonresponsive" on this ground without that process is the classic error.
- Moves a practitioner knows on this ground, most of which depend on what Corvane says in its response: (1) accept a C-16 held personally by the qualifying individual as satisfying the statute (one reading; contested); (2) condition the award on the C-16 classification being added to the firm's license before contract execution, or before the sprinkler phase, which does not begin until the summer 2027 shutdown; (3) substitution of a listed C-16 subcontractor under PCC § 4107/§ 4109 (necessity), with findings; (4) a responsibility hearing on the record, which also serves as leverage: a contractor rarely wants a public hearing on its faults, and neither does the Board. None of these is a Monday-morning instinct; the conditional award in particular follows the bidder's response rather than replacing the question.

**Ground 2, Addendum 2.** Lawful ground, and the District's choice. The failure to initial is a facial defect (ITB § 4.2). It is waivable as an immaterial irregularity: Item 1 is about $6,500 on a $13.4 million bid, the architect's log shows delivery to Corvane, and the bid form binds the bidder to "all Addenda." Waiving it with written findings is sound. Refusing to waive and rejecting Corvane on this ground alone is also lawful: an agency that may excuse an immaterial deviation is never required to, and a scope change the bidder may not have priced is the paradigm of a deviation the waiver test can refuse (MCM; Ghilotti; Konica). The exposure on rejection is not the law; it is the District's own record, if it has ever waived the same thing for another bidder. The harder question, if the District waives, is whether it can hold Corvane to the Addendum 2 work; the bid form's "including all Addenda" language is the answer.

**Ground 3, Copper Basin.** A responsibility question, never responsiveness. The District cannot reject on it without notice and a hearing. Dale's proposed call to the other district's CBO is a trap if it produces information the District acts on but the bidder never sees (Boydston); the sound form is to put what the other district knows in writing the bidder can answer, or to ask Corvane directly. (In fact the termination was rescinded by a 2025 settlement and recharacterized as a termination for convenience; that arrives with Corvane's response.)

**Timing.** Corvane's response is due Tuesday. The licensing ground turns on facts outside the bid, and the procedure gives Corvane two days to supply them. A recommendation written Monday morning is a recommendation that may change in front of the Board. Holding the recommendation until the response is in, and using the late supplement Tuesday morning, is the sound round-2 move; its cost is staff's patience and the clock. Asking the client what it wants (does it prefer the local bidder? is the $287,000 the priority? how much process is it willing to buy?) before committing to a path is the other thing an experienced lawyer does first.

**The menu, with the practitioner's verdict on each as a Monday recommendation.**
- Award to Corvane as is, deny the license ground on both trades, waive Addendum 2 with findings: risky. Half right; ignores § 7057(c) on the sprinklers.
- Reject Corvane as nonresponsive on both grounds, award Tasker & Moye: risky. The HVAC half is wrong on the statute; the sprinkler half rests on facts outside the bid and gets a hearing; "nonresponsive" is the wrong label.
- Hold the recommendation until Corvane's response is in; late supplement Tuesday: sound.
- Reject Corvane on Addendum 2 alone, award Tasker & Moye: defensible. Lawful; the exposure is the District's own waiver record.
- Reject all bids and rebid, with locksets in the base scope and a C-16 requirement on the prime or a listed sub: defensible. Broad discretion; its price is the calendar (award late November at the earliest, switchgear after December 1, electrical and HVAC to summer 2028), public prices, and the chance the same bidder is low again.
- Any path that rejects Corvane on Copper Basin, or on information from a phone call to Copper Basin, without notice and a hearing: unlawful.

**Smaller points.** Talbot's request for the bid file: bids are public records once opened; give her the file. The award can lawfully be carried to the next regular meeting or a special meeting, but the superintendent will not call a special meeting for a protest, and November 24 puts the switchgear release at the wire. A § 4109 substitution, a conditional award, and a responsibility hearing are all live options once Corvane's response is in; on Monday the sound advice names them as what may come next rather than choosing among them.
