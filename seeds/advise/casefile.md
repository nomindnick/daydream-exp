**8.1** Each Bidder shall complete the Statement of Qualifications attached to the Bid Form. The District may use the information in determining whether a Bidder is responsible.

## 21. Bid Protests

**21.1 Who may protest.** Only a Bidder that submitted a bid, and that would be in line for the award if its protest were upheld, may protest. A Bidder may not rely on another Bidder's protest.

**21.2 Deadline.** A protest must be in writing and must be received by the District's Director of Facilities and Construction, by email or hand delivery, no later than 5:00 p.m. on the third business day after the bid opening.

**21.3 Contents.** The protest must (a) state every ground for the protest and the facts that support it; (b) identify the provisions of the Bid Documents, statutes, or other authorities it relies on; (c) attach every document it relies on; (d) state the relief requested; and (e) give the name and telephone number of the protester's representative.

**21.4 Service.** When it files the protest, the protester must deliver a complete copy, with all attachments, to every other Bidder whose bid could be affected by the protest, including the apparent low Bidder.

**21.5 Response.** The Bidder whose bid is protested may submit a written response within two business days after it receives the protest.

**21.6 Review and decision.** The Superintendent or designee will review the protest and any response and may, in the District's discretion, meet with the protester and the protested Bidder. The Superintendent will make a written recommendation to the Board of Education, which will decide the protest when it considers the award. The Board's decision is final.

**21.7 No new grounds.** Grounds, arguments, and documents not included in a timely protest will not be considered.

**21.8 Exclusive remedy; waiver.** This procedure and its deadlines are mandatory and are each Bidder's sole and exclusive remedy for a bid protest. A protest that does not comply with this Section will be rejected. A Bidder that does not comply with this Section waives any right to pursue the protest further, including by Government Code claim or legal proceedings.

**21.9 No fee.** No bond, deposit, or fee is required to file a protest.

Dear Mr. Ruffin:

This firm represents Tasker & Moye Construction, Inc., the second-lowest bidder on Bid No. 26-04. Under Section 21 of the Instructions to Bidders, Tasker & Moye protests any award to the apparent low bidder, Corvane Builders, Inc. Corvane's bid is nonresponsive for two independent reasons, Corvane is not a responsible bidder, and Tasker & Moye is the lowest responsive and responsible bidder.

**1. Corvane is not licensed to perform the Work it bid.** The Work includes a new automatic fire sprinkler system in both buildings and the replacement of the rooftop HVAC units. Corvane holds a Class B general building license and nothing else (Exhibit A, the state license lookup). Its Designation of Subcontractors lists no fire sprinkler subcontractor and no HVAC subcontractor, and declares that all unlisted work "will be performed by the Bidder." A general building contractor may not contract for a project that includes a fire protection system unless it holds that classification or subcontracts the work to a contractor that does (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 7057, subd. (c); § 7026.12), and it may not perform specialty work outside its classification. Corvane cannot lawfully do the sprinkler work or the HVAC work itself, and it listed no one who can. A bid by a contractor not licensed for the work is nonresponsive and must be rejected (Bus. & Prof. Code, § 7028.15, subd. (e)). Tasker & Moye, by contrast, listed Riverbend Fire Protection (C-16) and Cinder Creek Mechanical (C-20).

> Section 7028.15, subdivision (e), leaves the District no discretion: "Unless one of the foregoing exceptions applies, a bid submitted to a public agency by a contractor who is not licensed in accordance with this chapter shall be considered nonresponsive and shall be rejected by the public agency. Unless one of the foregoing exceptions applies, a local public agency shall, before awarding a contract or issuing a purchase order, verify that the contractor was properly licensed when the contractor submitted the bid."

**2. Corvane did not acknowledge Addendum No. 2.** Addendum 2 changed the locksets on 14 classroom doors to the classroom security function, a student-safety requirement we estimate adds $18,000 to the work. Corvane's Bid Form acknowledges Addendum 1 only. Under ITB § 4.2, that failure makes the bid nonresponsive. An unacknowledged addendum also gives the bidder a choice no other bidder had: to argue later that it never agreed to the changed work, or to walk away.

**3. Corvane is not a responsible bidder.** In March 2024, Copper Basin Unified School District terminated Corvane's contract for the Copper Basin High School gymnasium modernization for cause, citing schedule failures and uncorrected rejected work, and its Board ratified the termination in open session (Exhibit B). A contractor terminated for cause by a neighboring school district within the last three years does not have the trustworthiness, quality, fitness, capacity, and experience the law requires of a responsible bidder.

**Relief requested.** Tasker & Moye asks that the District (a) reject Corvane's bid as nonresponsive; (b) in the alternative, find Corvane not a responsible bidder; (c) award the contract to Tasker & Moye as the lowest responsive and responsible bidder; and (d) before the Board acts, hold a hearing at which Tasker & Moye may present evidence and question Corvane's principals.

Tasker & Moye reserves all of its rights, including the right to seek a writ of mandate.
